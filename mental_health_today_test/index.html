Learn More About Checking In With Your Mental Health Today

A daily check-in looks at how you are doing right now — today's mood, energy, stress, and coping — rather than a broad pattern over weeks. Present-moment awareness like this is one of the simplest, most evidence-backed habits for catching strain early, before it builds into something bigger.

No single day defines your overall mental health, but noticing today clearly — what helped, what drained you, what you need before tomorrow — is a small habit with an outsized payoff.

Ways to check in with yourself day to day:

  • Name Today's State, Not Just "Fine": Try to identify the specific feeling (tired, on-edge, content, flat) rather than defaulting to a vague answer.
  • Track Patterns Across Several Days: A single hard day is normal; several hard days in a row, or a repeating dip at the same time each day, is worth paying attention to.
  • Build a Short Evening Reset: A few minutes of winding down — putting the phone away, a short walk, or simply naming one good moment from the day — helps close the day out more gently.
  • Know When Today Points to Something Bigger: If difficult days are becoming most days, or basic functioning is affected, a doctor or mental-health professional can help.
